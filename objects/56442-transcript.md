CENTENNIAL EXHIBITION

WITH A FULL DESCRIPTION OF THE GREAT

BUILDINGS AND ALL THE OBJECTS OF INTEREST IN THEM.

This work gives a complete and vivid description of the great Exhibition. The Author has written from his own personal knowledge, having gone through every part of the great Fair, notebook in hand, recording the vast and varied information contained in this book. He has received in his labors the constant and sympathetic assistance of the Centennial authorities, and it is the only Official History of the Centennial Exhibition offered to the public. It contains a complete account of the great Exhibition from its commencement to its close.

IT TREATS

Of the Exhibition grounds; giving an account of them, the manner in which they were laid off, and the location of every object of interest in them—the Buildings, Fountains, Lakes, Gardens, etc.

Of the great buildings of the Exhibition; giving their history, their size, the details of their construction, and an accurate description of each.

Of the Main Exhibition Building, that superb palace of glass and iron; of the rare and beautiful articles displayed within it; the rich Jewels, Laces, Silks, and manufactures of all kinds, which were here spread out in a manner that surpassed the wildest flights of romance; and the thousands of beautiful and curious objects with which the vast hall was filled to overflowing.

Of Machinery Hall, the grand temple of the Mechanic Arts; of the great Corliss Engine, and the thousands of machines which filled the hall, and exhibited the mechanical industries of the world.

Of Memorial Hall, the beautiful Art Gallery, with its thousands of Paintings, Statues, Bronzes, etc.; the most superb Art Collection ever gathered together in any Exhibition.

Of Agricultural Hall, in which was made the most complete display of the agricultural systems of the various nations of the world. This was the grand original feature of our Exhibition, and was a source of the deepest interest to all who visited it. To the farmer, the account of the superb display in this building is worth the price of the whole book.

Of Horticultural Hall, the fairy-like palace in which the flowers of the world were to be seen; of the beautiful landscape garden which surrounded it; and the exquisite specimens of rare and
beautiful plants which it contained.

Of the United States Government Building; in which were displayed the great National Museum and the practical workings of the Treasury, War, Navy, Interior, and Post-Office Departments. The account of the collections contained within this building is brilliantly written, and is of the deepest interest to every citizen of the Union.

Of the Woman’s Pavilion, with its beautiful collections of the work of woman in the various occupations in which her skill and patience have won her success. This department of the Exhibition is fully described in this work, and the account given of it cannot fail to interest the women of America.

Of the Buildings of the Various States of the Union, the arrangements. made for special displays, and for the comfort and convenience of the visitors from the various States; with a description of the buildings and their internal arrangements.

Of the Buildings of Foreign Countries; giving interesting descriptions of the stately mansions of Great Britain, the handsome pavilions of Spain and Germany, the curious residence and bazaars of the Japanese, the Cafés of the Turks and Tunisians; the bazaars of the Syrians, and tents of Bedouin Arabs.

This History is invaluable to all classes of our people. There is not another book in print which gives one-fourth of the information contained in this work. To those who have visited the Exhibition, it will be a pleasing souvenir of their visit, and will enable them to recall the magnificent scenes they have witnessed. To those who did not make the journey, the book is a necessity, for it will enable them to enjoy the delights of a thorough acquaintance with the great World’s Fair in the quietude of their own homes. With the certainty of an immense sale, the publishers have placed the price at a very low figure, so as to bring it within the reach of all classes, and enable every American citizen to possess a copy of this superb Memorial Volume of the International Centennial Exhibition.
